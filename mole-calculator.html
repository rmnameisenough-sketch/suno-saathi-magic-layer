📘 How It Works

A mole is a unit used to measure the amount of substance. One mole contains approximately 6.022 × 10²³ particles.

Grams = Moles × Molar Mass

Moles = Grams ÷ Molar Mass

Particles = Moles × Avogadro Constant

Example: 18 g of H₂O

  • Molar mass of water = 18.015 g/mol
  • Moles = 18 ÷ 18.015 ≈ 0.999 mol
  • Particles ≈ 0.999 × 6.022 × 10²³

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is Avogadro's number?

It is 6.02214076 × 10²³ particles per mole.

What is the difference between mole and particle?

A mole represents a fixed number of particles such as atoms, molecules or formula units.
