🚀 Conservation of Momentum

Calculate collision velocities and momentum using the law of conservation of momentum.

📘 Formula

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

Momentum: p = mv

Common velocity:

v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂) / (m₁ + m₂)
Velocity is signed: positive and negative values indicate opposite directions.

📝 Example

Object 1: mass 2 kg, velocity 5 m/s

Object 2: mass 3 kg, velocity −2 m/s

p = 2×5 + 3×(−2) = 4 kg·m/s

If both objects stick together:

v = 4 / 5 = 0.8 m/s

⚙️ How It Works

  • Enter both masses in kilograms.
  • Enter initial velocities in m/s with their direction signs.
  • Select the calculation you want.
  • For final velocity calculations, enter the other object's final velocity.
  • Press Calculate to get the result.

❓ FAQs

1. What is conservation of momentum?
Total momentum remains constant in an isolated system.

2. Can velocity be negative?
Yes. Negative velocity represents motion in the opposite direction.

3. What is a perfectly inelastic collision?
Objects stick together and move with a common velocity after collision.

4. What is the SI unit of momentum?
kg·m/s.
