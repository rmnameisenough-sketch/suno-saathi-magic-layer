🚀 Motion and Forces: Formulas and Calculators

This chapter group covers how objects move and why. Start with the equations of motion, then connect them to force and momentum. The calculators below show each substitution step so you can compare your own working with theirs.

The equations of motion above assume constant acceleration in a straight line.

Worked example

A car moving at 5 m/s accelerates uniformly at 3 m/s² for 4 s. Find its final velocity and the distance covered.

  1. Final velocity: v = u + at = 5 + 3 × 4 = 17 m/s.
  2. Distance: s = ut + ½at² = 5 × 4 + ½ × 3 × 4² = 20 + 24 = 44 m.
  3. Check with s = (u + v)t / 2 = (5 + 17) × 4 / 2 = 44 m. Both methods agree.

Try the same numbers in the calculators above to check each step.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing units: convert km/h to m/s by dividing by 3.6 before using the formulas.
  • Using v = u + at when the acceleration is not constant.
  • Forgetting the sign of acceleration. A slowing object has a negative a if the direction of motion is positive.
  • Entering the angle in radians when a calculator expects degrees (the projectile and torque pages use degrees).

Quick questions

When can I use the equations of motion?

When acceleration is constant and the motion is along a straight line. Projectile motion works because the horizontal and vertical parts can be treated separately.

Is impulse the same as momentum?

No. Momentum is mass times velocity. Impulse is the change in momentum, equal to force times the time it acts.
