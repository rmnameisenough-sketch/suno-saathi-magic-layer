🌡️ Properties of Matter, Fluids and Heat: Formulas and Calculators

This group covers bulk matter: how dense it is, how it pushes, how fluids flow and how heat changes temperature or does work. Unit care matters more here than almost anywhere else in physics.

Key formulas

What you findFormulaUnit
Densityρ = m / Vkg/m³
PressureP = F / APa
Pressure at depthP = ρghPa
Buoyant forceF_b = ρgVN
Flow rateQ = Avm³/s
Continuity equationA₁v₁ = A₂v₂m³/s

Heat energyQ = mcΔTJ
Ideal gas lawPV = nRTJ
Work at constant pressureW = PΔVJ
First law of thermodynamicsΔU = Q − WJ
Celsius to Fahrenheit°F = °C × 9/5 + 32°F
Celsius to KelvinK = °C + 273.15K

R = 8.314 J/(mol·K). In the ideal gas law, temperature must be in kelvin, and pressure and volume must be in consistent units (Pa and m³ for the calculator).

Worked example

How much heat is needed to warm 2 kg of water by 10 K (c = 4200 J/kg·K), and what is the pressure due to a 10 m column of water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)?

  1. Heat: Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4200 × 10 = 84,000 J.
  2. Pressure from the water column: P = ρgh = 1000 × 9.8 × 10 = 98,000 Pa.

Try the same numbers in the calculators above to check each step.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using °C in the ideal gas law. Always convert to kelvin first.
  • Mixing pressure units. 1 atm = 101,325 Pa.
  • Confusing a temperature change with a temperature. ΔT is the same in °C and K.
  • Using grams for mass while c is given in J/kg·K.

Quick questions

Why is the ΔT the same in Celsius and Kelvin?

Both scales have equal-sized degrees, so a difference of 10 °C equals a difference of 10 K.

What does a negative W mean in ΔU = Q − W?

Negative work done by the gas means work is done on the gas, which raises its internal energy.
