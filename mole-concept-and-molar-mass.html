⚗️ Mole Concept and Molar Mass: Formulas and Calculators

The mole links the invisible world of atoms to the grams you weigh on a balance. Almost every quantitative chemistry chapter later builds on these three relations.

Key formulas

What you findFormulaUnit
Molar mass of a compoundM = Σ(atomic mass × number of atoms)g/mol
Moles from massn = m / Mmol
Mass from molesm = n × Mg
Number of particlesN = n × N_Aparticles
Avogadro’s numberN_A ≈ 6.022 × 10²³mol⁻¹

Atomic masses come from the periodic table, which is why molar mass of water is about 18.015 g/mol rather than exactly 18.

Worked example

How many moles and molecules are in 9.0 g of water?

  1. Molar mass of H₂O: 2 × 1.008 + 15.999 ≈ 18.015 g/mol.
  2. Moles: n = 9.0 / 18.015 ≈ 0.4996 mol.
  3. Molecules: N = 0.4996 × 6.022 × 10²³ ≈ 3.01 × 10²³ molecules.

Try the same numbers in the calculators above to check each step.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to multiply the atomic mass by the subscript, or by the number outside brackets, as in Ca(OH)₂ where OH is counted twice.
  • Mixing up grams and moles in n = m / M.
  • Using atomic number instead of atomic mass.
  • Rounding molar mass too early, which changes the final answer in the third significant figure.

Quick questions

What does the molar mass calculator accept?

Chemical formulas with brackets, such as Ca(OH)2 or Al2(SO4)3. Type the formula using normal digits.

Why is Avogadro’s number so large?

Atoms are extremely small, so a handful of grams of any substance contains an enormous number of them.
