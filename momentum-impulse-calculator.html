Formulas Used

Momentum: p = mv

Impulse: J = FΔt

Impulse: J = Δp = p₂ − p₁

Force: F = Δp ÷ Δt

Final velocity: v₂ = v₁ + J/m

Momentum unit: kg·m/s | Impulse unit: N·s

How It Works

  • Momentum: Mass multiplied by velocity.
  • Impulse: Force acting over a time interval.
  • Momentum change: Final momentum minus initial momentum.
  • Force: Momentum change divided by time interval.

Velocity is directional. Enter negative velocity when motion is in the opposite direction.

Examples

Momentum: A 5 kg object moves at 4 m/s. p = 5 × 4 = 20 kg·m/s.

Impulse: A 10 N force acts for 2 seconds. J = 10 × 2 = 20 N·s.

Force: Momentum changes by 30 kg·m/s in 3 seconds. F = 30 ÷ 3 = 10 N.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the SI unit of momentum?

kg·m/s (kilogram metre per second).

What is impulse?

Impulse is the product of force and the time interval during which it acts. It equals the change in momentum.

Can momentum be negative?

Yes. Momentum has direction. Negative momentum indicates motion in the chosen negative direction.

What is the SI unit of impulse?

Newton-second (N·s), equivalent to kg·m/s.
