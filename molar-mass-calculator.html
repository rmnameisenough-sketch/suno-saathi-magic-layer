📘 How It Works

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance. It is calculated by adding the atomic masses of all atoms present in a chemical formula.

Formula

Molar Mass = Σ (Atomic Mass × Number of Atoms)

Example: H₂O

  • Hydrogen atomic mass = 1.008
  • Oxygen atomic mass = 15.999
  • Water contains 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom.
  • Molar mass = 2 × 1.008 + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is molar mass?

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance, expressed in grams per mole (g/mol).

How is molar mass calculated?

Multiply each element's atomic mass by its number of atoms and add all contributions.

Can I calculate compounds with brackets?

Yes. Formulas such as Ca(OH)₂ and Al₂(SO₄)₃ are supported.

Why does capitalization matter?

Chemical symbols are case-sensitive. CO₂ means carbon dioxide, while Co means cobalt.
