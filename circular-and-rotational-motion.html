🌀 Circular and Rotational Motion: Formulas and Calculators

Rotation mirrors straight-line motion: angle replaces distance, angular velocity replaces velocity, torque replaces force and moment of inertia replaces mass. Learning that mapping makes this chapter group much easier.

Worked example

A wheel starts with ω₀ = 2 rad/s and has a constant angular acceleration of 3 rad/s² for 4 s. Find ω and the angle turned.

  1. Final angular velocity: ω = ω₀ + αt = 2 + 3 × 4 = 14 rad/s.
  2. Angle turned: θ = ω₀t + ½αt² = 2 × 4 + ½ × 3 × 4² = 8 + 24 = 32 rad.

Try the same numbers in the calculators above to check each step.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing rpm and rad/s. 60 rpm is 2π rad/s, not 60 rad/s.
  • Treating centripetal force as an extra force. It is the net inward force provided by tension, gravity, friction or another real force.
  • Using the wrong moment of inertia for the shape. I = mr² applies to a point mass or a thin ring about its axis.
  • Using degrees where radians are required in θ = ω₀t + ½αt².

Quick questions

What provides the centripetal force for a planet?

Gravity. The gravitational pull of the Sun supplies the inward force that keeps a planet in orbit.

Why does a figure skater spin faster when pulling arms in?

Angular momentum L = Iω stays constant, so reducing the moment of inertia I increases the angular velocity ω.
