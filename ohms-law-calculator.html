Formulas Used

Voltage: V = IR

Current: I = V ÷ R

Resistance: R = V ÷ I

Power: P = VI

Power: P = I²R

Power: P = V² ÷ R

Voltage: Volt (V) | Current: Ampere (A) | Resistance: Ohm (Ω) | Power: Watt (W)

How It Works

  • Voltage: Electrical potential difference across a component.
  • Current: Rate of flow of electric charge.
  • Resistance: Opposition to the flow of electric current.
  • Power: Rate at which electrical energy is transferred.

Ohm's law applies to ohmic components when physical conditions such as temperature remain constant.

Examples

Voltage: Current = 2 A, Resistance = 5 Ω. V = 2 × 5 = 10 V.

Current: Voltage = 12 V, Resistance = 4 Ω. I = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.

Resistance: Voltage = 12 V, Current = 2 A. R = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 Ω.

Power: Voltage = 12 V, Current = 2 A. P = 12 × 2 = 24 W.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Ohm's law?

Ohm's law states that voltage equals current multiplied by resistance under constant physical conditions.

What is the unit of resistance?

Resistance is measured in Ohms (Ω).

What is electrical power?

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred, measured in watts.

Can resistance be zero?

This calculator requires resistance greater than zero when dividing by resistance. Ideal zero-resistance conditions are not handled by these formulas.
